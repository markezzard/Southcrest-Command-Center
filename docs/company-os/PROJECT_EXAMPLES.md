### Story in stages (as described)

#### 1) Introduction / pitch
- Introduced to Mason board and executive leadership (David Hull).
- Met board and financing representative (Ike).
- Pitched Meridian services: find land, approvals, permits; then Southcrest can develop/build.
- They agreed → land search began.

#### 2) Land search setup
- Met school to define the geographic search boundary.
- Pulled every property that was permitted-by-right for a charter school inside that boundary.
- Analyzed candidates for traffic, wetlands, and other development conditions.
- Zoning insight: in Collier County, charter schools can be approved in state zoning areas, which opened residential property as targets.
- Residential/estate sites were often cheaper but frequently required **assemblage** of multiple parcels and aligning multiple sellers.

#### 3) Outreach and targeting
- Selected target sites from the filtered list (single parcels and multi-parcel assemblages).
- Meridian ran extensive outreach: calls, emails, letters, texts.
- Goal of outreach: create seller interest, then move toward LOIs.

#### 4) Site control — Vanderbilt Road assemblage
- Secured interest on **four adjacent sites** on Vanderbilt Road (location described as superb).
- Four willing sellers.
- Structured **four identical deals** covering due diligence, government approvals, and closing.
- Negotiated and executed all contracts.

#### 5) Due diligence
- About **90 days** of due diligence after contracts.

#### 6) Government approvals / permitting
- Remained in government approvals until key permits were obtained, including:
  - ERP (Environmental Resource Permit)
  - SDP (Site Development Permit)
  - FDEP offsite permits
  - Major offsite work: traffic light and large intersection improvements on a four-lane road
- Site challenges: very wet; existing wetlands requiring mitigation.
- Described as complicated approvals package.

#### 7) Southcrest construction / delivery
- After approvals/permits, started site work, completed building, turned over to client.
- Construction process included:
  - bidding
  - RFIs
  - submittals (spoken as “middles”; confirm term)
  - additional construction-side permitting
  - about **11 months** of construction
  - daily logs, construction schedule, pay apps, and related project controls

## What this example teaches the system

1. Full Meridian → Southcrest school delivery is not a short funnel; it is a multi-year staged machine.
2. Email/text/letter outreach is core Meridian work product, not side noise.
3. Assemblage means Project Master must handle many parcels and sellers under one project identity.
4. “Permitted” is a package of permits and offsite conditions, not a single checkbox.
5. Construction has its own information world (bidding, RFIs, submittals, daily logs, pay apps, schedule) that later agents must respect.
6. Org roles are stable enough to model even when named people on a job change.
